About OWW 尾張

Ability is everywhere. Opportunity is not.

Everyone is unusually good at something, and everyone works inside circumstances they did not choose. One person has rare skill and no setting in which to use it. Elsewhere a business has the demand, the budget, and nobody to do the work. Most of the time nothing connects the two, and the imbalance is accepted as simply how things are.

We think information technology has become capable enough to change that. So we build the systems that let talent and resources be shared across companies, languages, and borders, and we treat every such connection as a business rather than a gesture: new work, new markets, and revenue none of the participants could have reached alone.

Owari’s karakuri puppets and the Toyoda family’s looms are different chapters of one region’s history, connected by the challenge of making clever ideas perform reliably in the real world. We bring that challenge into IT. A product must fit the people using it, remain dependable in daily operation, and be maintainable as their needs change.

We take responsibility for technical complexity so our customers and partners can concentrate on their work.

  • Karakuri puppets

    Concealed mechanisms, graceful movement

    Owari's karakuri turned intricate arrangements of springs and gears into movements an audience could appreciate immediately.

  • Toyoda looms

    Quality built into the machine

    Looms that stopped when a thread broke helped lay the foundations of a major automotive business.

  • Our workshop

    Engineers and customers, side by side

    We ask questions, build working versions, test assumptions, and improve the result until it earns its place in the business.

How we work

Listen first. Build something real. Keep improving it.

Five stages, in this order every time. The early ones cost the least and decide the most.

  1. Listen

    We begin by listening to the people who do the work.

  2. Agree

    Together we agree on the problem, scope, and intended outcome.

  3. Build & try

    A focused working version you can try and discuss, shaped by your feedback, tests, and review.

  4. Prepare & launch

    Before launch, we prepare the people who will use and maintain the system.

  5. Review & support

    After launch, we review its performance and agree on the support and improvements it needs.

Throughout the relationship, we explain progress and tradeoffs in plain language, including changes that affect cost or delivery.

Creating with the world

Global collaboration is the business, not a side benefit.

Japanese craftspeople once adapted imported Western clockwork into local clocks and karakuri mechanisms, making something new out of borrowed knowledge. We work the same way, with one difference: the exchange now runs in every direction at once, and we build it deliberately instead of leaving it to chance.

Ability, wherever it sits

We work with engineers, researchers, and specialists in other countries. Written handover, shared tooling, and genuinely overlapping hours are part of the design, not afterthoughts.

Resources held in common

We respect the work behind open-source software, follow its licenses, and contribute improvements when we can. Shared tools and shared knowledge let a small team reach well beyond its own size.

Built for more than one market

Language, accessibility, and local working practice belong in the design from the start, not in a translation pass at the end.

Our Nagoya roots give us a point of view. Sharing it, and taking in what others know, is what keeps it useful.
